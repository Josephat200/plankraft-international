Our Practice

What We Do

At Plankraft International, we are a studio defined by the full spectrum of the built environment. Our work begins where ideas are born and carries through to the final detail on-site — a seamless continuum of design thinking and technical precision.

We bring together architecture, interiors, urban strategy, project management, and environmental responsibility under one roof. Each discipline informs the other, ensuring that every project we deliver is coherent, considered, and enduring.

01
Architectural Design

From the first sketch to construction documentation, we craft buildings that are functional, contextual, and unmistakably purposeful. Our architectural process is driven by a deep understanding of programme, place, and the people who will inhabit each space — balancing aesthetic ambition with technical rigour at every stage.

02
Interior Design

We shape the experience within. Our interior design work spans commercial, hospitality, and bespoke residential environments — each one conceived with a singular attention to material, light, and human scale. We treat every interior as a complete composition, where form and feeling are inseparable.

03
Masterplanning

Great places begin with great planning. We approach masterplanning as a discipline of vision — organising land, movement, and activity into frameworks that communities can grow within. Our plans are strategic and adaptive, designed to remain relevant as the needs of users and cities evolve over time.

04
Project Management

Exceptional design deserves exceptional delivery. Our project management service bridges the gap between design intent and built reality — coordinating consultants, contractors, and timelines with clarity and discipline. We manage scope, cost, and programme across all project phases, ensuring that every commitment made at the design stage is honoured on the ground.

05
Green Buildings

Sustainability is not an afterthought in our practice — it is embedded in how we think from day one. We design buildings that are energy-conscious, climate-responsive, and built to last. By integrating passive design strategies, responsible material selection, and emerging green technologies, we create spaces that are good for people and for the planet.

Built in Kenya — For Kenya

Architecture that shapes us.

A curated celebration of Kenya’s architectural identity — from Nairobi’s evolving skyline and the ancient coral towns of the Coast, to the award-winning eco-lodges of the Rift Valley and Maasai Mara. These landmarks inspire our work and deepen our commitment to design rooted in Kenyan place, climate, and culture.

Civic
Kenyatta International Conference Centre
Karl Henrik Nostvik · 1973Nairobi CBD, Kenya

Kenya’s most iconic tower — a 28-storey drum-and-saucer composition that has defined the Nairobi skyline since independence. The revolving restaurant and helipad atop its cylindrical crown remain landmarks of post-colonial modernism in East Africa.

High-Rise
Nairobi Upperhill District
Various Architects · 2005–presentUpperhill, Nairobi

Africa’s fastest-growing commercial precinct. Upperhill’s curtain-wall towers — including the UAP Old Mutual Tower and Times Tower — reflect Kenya’s emergence as the region’s premier business hub, rising above the forest canopy of the Nairobi National Park.

Cultural
Nairobi National Museum
Somers & Partners · 1929 & 2008Museum Hill, Nairobi

Refurbished and expanded by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, the museum’s contemporary wing wraps a new glass atrium around the colonial-era core. It anchors the museum hill cultural precinct and houses East Africa’s most significant natural history and art collections.

Heritage
Fort Jesus
João Batista Cairato · 1593Mombasa Island, Kenya

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, Fort Jesus is one of the finest examples of 16th-century Portuguese military architecture. Built of coral rag on the Mombasa headland, its bastions and angular plan reflect the geometric principles of the Italian architect Matteo Cipolla — an enduring symbol of the Swahili Coast’s layered history.

Heritage
Lamu Old Town
Swahili craftsmen · 13th century onwardsLamu Island, Kenya

East Africa’s oldest living town and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Lamu’s coral-stone architecture — with its elaborately carved wooden doors, inner courtyards, and natural ventilation strategies — represents one of the world’s most sophisticated pre-industrial climate-responsive building traditions.

Commercial
Two Rivers Mall
Boogertman + Partners · 2017Runda, Nairobi

East and Central Africa’s largest mall at its opening — a 67,000 m² mixed-use development set along the Kiambu Road corridor. Its design integrates open-air plazas, water features, and generous daylighting, drawing on Kenyan landscape and climate to create a destination rather than a box.

Hospitality
Angama Mara Eco-Lodge
Silvio Rech & Lesley Carstens · 2015Maasai Mara, Kenya

Suspended 300 metres above the Mara on the Great Rift Valley escarpment, Angama Mara’s twin camps use tented glass-walled suites that dissolve the boundary between interior and landscape. Its architecture won international acclaim for environmental sensitivity and contextual design in a fragile ecosystem.

Institutional
Aga Khan Academy, Nairobi
Dar al-Handasah Architects · 2003Westlands, Nairobi

A flagship educational campus on 40 acres of Gigiri forest. The academy’s buildings are arranged along natural contours, with shaded colonnades, planted roofs, and passive ventilation systems designed for Nairobi’s equatorial climate — a model of how institutional architecture can respect landscape and community.

Commercial
Westlands Commercial Hub
Various Architects · 1990–2020Westlands, Nairobi

The Westlands district has evolved into one of Africa’s most dynamic commercial corridors. Delta House, Pinetree Centre, and the Parkside Towers demonstrate how Nairobi’s private sector has driven a distinctly Kenyan vocabulary of curtain wall and mixed-use podium architecture adapted for equatorial light and monsoon rains.

Residential
Karen Residential Villas
Various Architects · 2000–presentKaren, Nairobi

Nairobi’s leafy Karen suburb — named after Karen Blixen — has become the setting for some of Kenya’s finest contemporary residential architecture. Stone-clad homes with wide verandahs, planted courtyards, and natural swimming ponds reflect a distinctly Kenyan aesthetic that balances privacy, nature, and indoor-outdoor living.

Interiors
Kenyan Contemporary Interiors
Kenyan Design Studios · 2010–presentNairobi, Kenya

A new generation of Nairobi-based interior designers has built a global reputation for spaces that weave together Kenyan craftsmanship, natural materials — sisal, olive wood, hand-woven textiles — and contemporary spatial sensibility. Their work is increasingly commissioned from London to New York.

Green
Nairobi Green Building Standard
Kenya Green Building Society · 2011–presentNairobi, Kenya

Kenya is East Africa’s leader in green building certification. From solar-powered schools in Kibera to LEED-certified corporate campuses in Upperhill, Kenyan architects and developers are demonstrating that environmental responsibility and commercial success are not in conflict — they are inseparable.

Infrastructure
Nairobi Expressway
China Road & Bridge Corp. · 2022Nairobi, Kenya

The 27-kilometre elevated Nairobi Expressway — connecting JKIA to Westlands — is the largest infrastructure project in Kenya’s history. Its sinuous elevated deck, designed to minimise land acquisition while threading through a dense urban fabric, has reshaped the experience of moving through East Africa’s largest city.

Cultural
Bomas of Kenya
Government of Kenya · 1971Langata, Nairobi

A cultural village designed to preserve and celebrate the diverse homestead traditions of Kenya’s 42-plus ethnic communities. The main Harambee Arena — with its 3,500-seat open-air amphitheatre — reflects a deliberate post-independence architectural agenda to honour indigenous spatial and material cultures.

Hospitality
Rift Valley Tented Lodges
Various Architects · 1990–presentRift Valley & Laikipia, Kenya

Kenya’s world-renowned safari lodge tradition has become a benchmark for luxury eco-architecture globally. From Laikipia’s Ol Malo to Lewa’s Ekorian’s Mugie, these structures — raised on slender steel legs, clad in canvas and local timber — achieve near-zero footprint while creating some of the world’s most extraordinary guest experiences.

Civic
Kenya Affordable Housing Programme
Various Architects · 2018–presentNairobi & Nationwide, Kenya

Kenya’s 500,000-unit affordable housing agenda is reshaping how architects think about mass housing. Projects in Park Road Ngara, Starehe, and Shauri Moyo demonstrate that dignified, well-designed homes at scale are achievable with the right integration of community input, modular construction, and thoughtful landscape planning.

The Practice

Who We Are

Plankraft International is a design practice founded on the conviction that architecture is one of the most consequential acts of human creativity. We are a lean, focused studio — built on talent, not scale — where each project receives the full weight of our collective expertise and personal investment.

We operate at the intersection of design rigour and practical delivery, bridging the conceptual and the constructed with equal fluency. Our team brings diverse strengths to every commission: from the precision of BIM workflows to the tactile craft of physical model-making, from the sweep of strategic planning to the discipline of on-site execution.

We believe the best architecture emerges from curiosity, collaboration, and an uncompromising commitment to craft — and we bring all three to every project we touch.
